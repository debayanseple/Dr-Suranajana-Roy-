## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Oral Cancer Surgery
**Q: What does oral cancer surgery involve?**
**A:** This procedure involves surgically removing cancerous tumors or lesions from the mouth, tongue, or lips. It is often combined with reconstructive techniques to help restore normal speaking and swallowing functions.

### Head & Neck Cancer Surgery
**Q: What specific areas do these surgeries treat?**
**A:** These surgeries address malignant tumors located in the throat, voice box (larynx), salivary glands, sinuses, and the lymph nodes in the neck.

### Facial Fracture Surgery
**Q: How are facial fractures repaired?**
**A:** Surgeons carefully realign broken bones in the face—such as the jaw, cheekbones, or eye sockets—and typically secure them using small, specialized titanium plates and screws to ensure proper healing and facial symmetry.

### Jaw Cysts & Tumors Surgeries
**Q: Are jaw cysts and tumors always cancerous?**
**A:** No, the majority of jaw cysts and tumors are benign (non-cancerous). However, they are usually removed to prevent them from expanding and causing damage to the surrounding jawbone or displacing healthy teeth.

### Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) Surgeries
**Q: When is TMJ surgery recommended?**
**A:** Surgery on the temporomandibular joint is typically reserved for severe cases involving chronic jaw pain, restricted movement, or significant structural damage, usually after non-surgical treatments like physical therapy or mouthguards have failed.

### Wisdom Tooth Removal
**Q: Why do wisdom teeth often need to be extracted?**
**A:** Wisdom teeth are frequently removed because most jaws lack the space to accommodate them. This can cause the teeth to become impacted (trapped in the gums), infected, or to emerge at awkward angles that crowd or damage adjacent teeth.
